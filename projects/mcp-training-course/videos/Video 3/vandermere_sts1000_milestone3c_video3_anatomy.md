## 1. Lesson Overview

### Course

**STS-1000 Sales Readiness**

### Lesson Title

**Anatomy of the Stabilizer**

### Lesson Type

Core product architecture / subsystem overview

### Recommended Runtime

**3:00–3:30** if produced as a single edited lesson.  
The target is slightly longer than Videos 1 and 2 because this module introduces six named subsystems.

If necessary, the video can be trimmed to 2:45 by shortening the subsystem explanations and using more visual callouts instead of narration.

### Recommended Production Format

A stitched multi-segment HeyGen video composed of short generated clips, edited together with full-screen product visuals, labeled diagrams, close-up stills, and occasional avatar-led transitions.

### Primary Avatar

Polished Vandermere sales enablement trainer.

### Secondary Avatar

Optional Vandermere engineering specialist, preferably used for one short technical aside explaining that reps should not over-describe internal mechanics during customer conversations.

### Visual Style

This should be the most product-heavy video so far.

Recommended balance:

- 20% avatar-led narration
- 60% product visuals and labeled diagrams
- 15% close-up component shots
- 5% transition/title cards

### Absurdity Level

Moderate to elevated, but controlled.

The product can now feel more unusual than it did in Video 2, because the learner has already accepted the premise. Still, this lesson should not introduce the HTA-700 helium system. That late reveal belongs in the deployment module.

### Primary Lesson Goal

By the end of this lesson, sales professionals should be able to identify the six primary STS-1000 subsystems and connect each one to a business problem customers recognize.

This lesson should not attempt to make the learner an engineer. It should teach just enough subsystem fluency for credible sales conversations.

---

# 2. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, learners should be able to:

1. Identify the six primary STS-1000 subsystems.
2. Explain each subsystem in one sales-friendly sentence.
3. Connect each subsystem to a customer-facing business problem.
4. Distinguish visual product anatomy from deeper technical mechanics.
5. Avoid overexplaining pseudo-technical details during customer conversations.

---

# 3. Core Teaching Message

The STS-1000 is designed around six stabilization subsystems.

Each subsystem addresses a different enterprise failure mode:

| Subsystem | Stabilizes | Customer Pain |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Core Logic Reservoir | institutional logic | operational shock and drift |
| Dual Thermal Reasoning Coils | decision temperature | heated cross-functional decisions |
| Adaptive Throughput Governors | workflow pressure | bottlenecks and approval stalls |
| Preemptive Harmonic Diffuser | procedural rhythm | micro-inefficiencies and delay cascades |
| Inference Manifold | early warning | hidden bottlenecks and future delay |
| Causality Baffles | consequence containment | one decision contaminating unrelated workflows |

Sales reps do not need to describe the internal physics in detail. They need to connect each component to a recognizable business pain.

The Core Logic Reservoir is the central stabilizing element of the STS-1000.

During onboarding, Vandermere engineers calibrate the reservoir using the customer’s historical operating patterns: approval timing, escalation behavior, ERP telemetry, handoff records, and meeting density.

The Dual Thermal Reasoning Coils regulate decision temperature.

The coils draw excess heat out of these high-friction decision points, allowing approvals to continue moving through the business at a safe operating temperature.

For sales teams, this subsystem is especially relevant in quote-to-cash, procurement, invoice review, and support escalation conversations.

The governors keep work from piling up in the wrong place.

# 10. Overlay Text and Motion Notes

## General Overlay Style

Use restrained, corporate technical overlays.

Recommended style:

- small uppercase labels
- thin leader lines
- subtle blue highlights
- amber warnings only for emphasis
- minimal motion
- no playful animations
- no neon sci-fi UI clutter

## Label Treatment

For the main anatomy diagram, do not rely on generated text. Add labels in editing or in the slide layer.

Preferred label format:

**01 CORE LOGIC RESERVOIR**  
Stabilizes institutional logic

**02 THERMAL REASONING COILS**  
Regulate decision temperature

And so on.

## Transition Ideas

Use these sparingly:

- mechanical iris wipe
- subtle horizontal scan line
- callout line draw-on
- soft cut with low mechanical click
- slow push-in on still images
- diagram label fade-in
- blue line stabilization transition

Avoid:

- glitch effects
- comedy transitions
- whoosh-heavy motion graphics
- sci-fi hologram effects
- spinning 3D text

## Audio Ideas

Optional, but useful if you are editing segments together:

- low mechanical room tone
- subdued industrial hum under product close-ups
- soft click when a callout appears
- low pulse for Thermal Reasoning Coils
- faint rotor hum for Harmonic Diffuser
- no comedic stingers

Keep audio extremely restrained. The project should feel premium, not like a parody trailer.

What is the best way for sales professionals to explain STS-1000 subsystems during early customer conversations?

### Answers

A. Begin with the internal mechanics of each subsystem and describe the full stabilization sequence in technical detail.

B. Start with the customer instability, then connect that pain to the relevant subsystem.
