# 2. Lesson Purpose

This lesson introduces the STS-1000 sales readiness campaign to internal Vandermere employees.

The audience already knows Vandermere Applied Dynamics, so the lesson should not spend time on founder history or broad corporate lore. Instead, it should explain why the STS-1000 matters now, what the course will cover, and what sales professionals are expected to learn.

The lesson sets up the STS-1000 as a major market expansion opportunity and frames the course as a practical readiness path for selling a complex capital infrastructure product.

---

# 3. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, learners should be able to:

1. Explain why Vandermere is launching a focused STS-1000 sales readiness campaign.
2. Describe the STS-1000 as a flagship product within Vandermere’s Physicalized Workflow Architecture portfolio.
3. Understand the course structure at a high level.
4. Recognize the importance of positioning the STS-1000 as infrastructure rather than software.
5. Identify the main sales-readiness outcomes expected from the course.

---

# 4. Key Teaching Message

The STS-1000 is moving into broader market readiness.

Sales professionals do not need to become field engineers, but they do need to explain the product clearly, qualify the right customers, understand the business value, and avoid reducing the product to familiar but incorrect software language.

# 13. Glossary Terms Used in This Lesson

## Physicalized Workflow Architecture

Vandermere’s product category for enterprise machinery that stabilizes institutional workflow behavior at the physical layer.

## Synaptic Throughput Stabilizer STS-1000

Vandermere’s flagship kinetic enterprise ballast and the focus of the sales readiness course.

## Workflow Stabilization

The preservation of safe operating conditions around enterprise workflows, including pressure, temperature, drift, and continuity.

## Operational Drift

The gradual separation between stated business priorities and actual day-to-day execution.

## Handoff Decay

The loss of clarity, ownership, or momentum as work moves between departments.

## Market Readiness

The sales organization’s ability to explain, position, qualify, and support customer conversations around a new product introduction.
